TikTok Shop rejects any product image below 600x600 pixels, and that floor hasn't moved in the 2026 spec update. But clearing the upload gate isn't the same as getting your listing seen. TikTok Shop's own seller guidance points toward 800x800px or higher, with 1000x1000px called out as the resolution that renders cleanly across feed, search, and live shopping placements. A 601px image technically passes. It also looks soft next to a competitor's 1200px listing in the same scroll.

Here's how to go from a barely-passing image to a batch that clears both the automated check and the visibility threshold. Building the workflow the first time takes 20-30 minutes; after that, with a saved template, it's under five minutes per product. Below: what breaks before you even open an editor, the steps that take a raw photo to a compliant file, how to verify each step, and the failure modes that cause rejections even above 600px.

## What You Need Before You Upload

Start with a source photo shot well above the 600px floor, ideally at least 2000px on the shortest side straight out of the camera or phone. Shooting low and upscaling later introduces blur that TikTok Shop's automated quality review can flag even when the raw pixel count technically clears the gate.

You also need an editor that can batch resize and export under TikTok Shop's 10MB file cap. The [TikTok Shop Image Checker](/tools/tiktok-shop-image) checks that cap alongside both the 600px US floor and the 800px UK floor, which sellers running cross-border listings often mix up.

Most sellers skip checking the color profile, and it's the step that causes the most confusing rejections. Images saved with embedded Adobe RGB or CMYK profiles sometimes render oversaturated or trigger a re-encode flag on upload. Convert everything to sRGB before you export.

Skip setting a fixed template canvas, like 1200x1200px, and every export comes out a slightly different size. That makes batch verification a headache and produces inconsistent crops once TikTok's feed and zoom views apply their own cropping logic on top of your file.

## The Four Phases to Hit the 1000px Standard

Each phase below produces one concrete, checkable output. Run them in order, and don't move on until the current one's checkpoint passes.

### Phase 1: Audit your source files

Open your product photo folder and check every file's pixel dimensions using your OS file properties panel (right-click > Properties on Windows, or Get Info on Mac). Flag anything under 1000px on the shortest side for a reshoot or an upscale review. Files between 600 and 999px will clear TikTok Shop's upload gate but sit below the recommended rendering size, which hurts how sharp the image looks in a crowded feed.

Checkpoint: every file reports at least 1000px on its shortest side, with no corrupted or zero-byte files in the folder.

### Phase 2: Standardize background and crop

Crop each product to fill roughly 60-70% of the frame and apply a solid, neutral background. A square 1:1 crop matches TikTok Shop's dominant main-image aspect ratio and avoids the feed auto-cropping a rectangular photo in a way you didn't intend. If your source shots have inconsistent backdrops, grey seamless paper, cluttered tables, visible shadows, you'll need to cut the product out cleanly before standardizing.

This is where browser-based checkers hit their limit. A checker can tell you a file passes the pixel count, but it can't cut a product out of a grey background for you. [AI Ecom Visual Studio](/tools/ai-ecom-visual-studio) handles that part on desktop, so you're not manually masking each photo in a separate editor before resizing.

Checkpoint: every image in the batch shows a consistent background color and a square crop when opened side by side in a folder preview.

### Phase 3: Resize and export at the 1000px target

Set your canvas to 1000x1000px, or go to 1200x1200px for safe headroom, and export as JPEG at 80-90% quality to balance file size against the 10MB cap. Batch rename files using your SKU convention so they match your upload sheet without manual renaming later.

Checkpoint: exported files open at exactly your chosen square dimension and sit well under 2MB each, comfortably inside the cap.

### Phase 4: Batch verify before upload

Run the full batch through the [TikTok Shop Image Checker](/tools/tiktok-shop-image) to confirm the 600px US floor, the 800px UK floor, and the 10MB file cap all pass. TikTok Shop's own seller university material lays out the current image requirements sellers are expected to follow before publishing.

Checkpoint: every file shows a pass status before you add it to your product listing upload, with zero flags on size, format, or file weight.

## Failure Modes: Why Images Still Get Rejected

A file clearing 600px can still fail for reasons that have nothing to do with pixel count. The most common one: an image rejected despite being well over 600px, which is usually a corrupted color profile or a progressive JPEG encoding that TikTok's system can't parse cleanly. Re-export as a baseline JPEG in sRGB, not progressive, and it typically clears.

Another pattern shows up after upload, not before it: the image goes live but looks pixelated inside the app. That's what happens when you ship a file sitting right at 600px and TikTok's own compression compresses it again on top. Never export at the floor  -  treat 1000px as your working minimum, not your target.

Cropping causes its own headaches. A non-square source image gets auto-cropped by TikTok's display logic in ways you didn't choose, which is why pre-cropping to 1:1 before export matters more than it seems like it should. And in batch uploads specifically, mixed formats (a stray PNG next to a folder of JPEGs) or inconsistent color profiles across the batch can cause files to get silently skipped rather than flagged.

Standardize format and profile across the whole batch before you touch the upload screen, and this one mostly disappears.

Beyond pure spec issues, TikTok Shop's automated review also checks whether the listing image matches the item actually shipped. Heavily retouched or synthetic-looking product shots that diverge from the real product can get flagged during review, separate from any size or format problem. If you're using AI enhancement, keep it close to what a customer will receive.

## FAQ

### Is 600x600px enough for TikTok Shop in 2026?

It's enough to clear the upload gate, but TikTok Shop's own guidance recommends 800x800px or higher, with 1000x1000px cited as the resolution that renders best across feed, search, and live placements. Treat 600px as the floor you never go below, not the target you aim for.

### What is the best aspect ratio for TikTok Shop product images?

A square 1:1 crop is the safest choice for the main listing image, since it's the dominant format TikTok Shop's feed and search displays handle without auto-cropping your photo in unexpected ways.

### Why is my TikTok Shop image being rejected despite meeting the 600px minimum?

Rejections above 600px usually come from a non-standard color profile, a progressive JPEG encoding, a mismatched file format inside a batch upload, or an image that doesn't visually match the item that ships. Pixel count passing doesn't rule out these other causes.

### Do I need a solid white background for all TikTok Shop images?

A solid, neutral background improves consistency and avoids visual clutter, but it's not a strict requirement for every image slot. It matters most for the primary listing photo, where a clean background helps the product stand out in a feed.
