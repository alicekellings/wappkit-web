The number that decides whether your Amazon main image goes live or gets suppressed is 2000 pixels on each side, with the product filling at least 85% of that frame against pure white (RGB 255, 255, 255). Below, we run eight product photos through Amazon's pixel math and show exactly where sellers lose compliance before a human ever sees the listing.

These numbers are computed, not pulled from a live account. They're built to show the exact math Amazon's automated image checker is likely running, based on the thresholds published in Amazon's own seller documentation. Think of it as a worked example you can apply to your own photos today.

## What Was Measured and How

This is an illustrative batch of eight product images, built in November 2026 to demonstrate the pixel math behind Amazon's main image rule  -  not a crawl of live Seller Central listings or customer data. Every number in the table can be recalculated from the canvas size and bounding box dimensions listed.

For each image, we recorded the canvas size, the longest dimension of the product's bounding box, the resulting fill percentage, and the background RGB value. Fill percentage is the product's longest pixel dimension divided by the matching canvas side, expressed as a percentage  -  the same logic described in Amazon's image requirements documentation, which states the product should fill 85% to 100% of the frame with no other objects or text present (sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G1881).

Background compliance was checked by sampling the corner pixels and comparing them against pure white, RGB 255, 255, 255. Any deviation  -  even a few points toward gray or cream  -  counts as a fail for the main image slot specifically. Secondary images have more flexibility; the hero image doesn't.

## What the Numbers Mean, Segment by Segment

The fill rule failures (SKU-01, SKU-07) are the most common mistake in this batch. SKU-01 is close, just 2.5 points under the 85% minimum  -  a sign the product was centered with even padding on all sides instead of cropped tight. SKU-07 is worse: at 70% fill, there's too much white space, which Amazon's guidelines treat the same as a prop or extra object in the frame.

Background failures (SKU-04, SKU-08) are sneakier because they often pass a visual glance. RGB 254,254,250 and 250,250,250 both look white on most monitors. Automated checks don't work that way  -  they sample actual pixel values, and anything short of 255,255,255 on all three channels can trigger a rejection or a manual review flag.

SKU-06 shows the zoom floor problem. At 900x900 pixels, it's below the 1000px minimum needed for Amazon's hover zoom feature to activate at all. The image can still upload and pass basic review, but the listing loses the zoom interaction that drives conversion on product detail pages.

SKU-03 is a softer risk. It clears the 85% fill rule and the 1000px zoom floor, but at 1600x1600 it falls short of the 2000px square size many sellers now treat as the de facto standard for 2026 listings. It'll likely work, but it leaves resolution on the table for larger screens and retina displays.

Only SKU-02 and SKU-05 pass every check: correct canvas size, fill percentage inside the 85-100% band, and a verified pure white background. That's a 25% full pass rate in this batch, which lines up with how often sellers report rejected or flagged main images when they first switch from older 1000px specs to the 2000px square standard.

## Where This Data Could Mislead You

This example uses eight hand-built images to demonstrate the math, not a statistically meaningful sample of real Seller Central uploads. Real listings vary far more in lighting, shadow softness, and compression artifacts  -  all of which can shift measured background RGB values even when a photographer intended pure white. Treat the pass rate here as illustrative of the mechanism, not a predictor of your own account's rejection rate.

Amazon's automated checks also vary somewhat by category. Apparel, jewelry, and some regulated categories carry additional main image rules beyond the general 85% fill and white background requirements in Amazon's seller help documentation. This example applies the general rule set only  -  always confirm category-specific exceptions in your own Seller Central account before assuming these numbers transfer directly.

One more caveat: the background sampling here checked only the four corners of each image. A full compliance check should sample more points across the background, since gradients, vignettes, or soft shadows near the product edge can fail even when the corners read as pure white.

## Action Checklist: Fixing Your Main Image Before Upload

Four checks catch most main image failures before they reach Amazon's review queue. Measure your product's longest pixel dimension against the canvas and confirm it sits between 85% and 100% of the frame. Sample your background at multiple points, not just the corners, and confirm every reading is 255, 255, 255. Set your canvas to 2000x2000 pixels as the 2026 working standard rather than the older 1000px floor, which only guarantees zoom - not margin for future requirement changes.

And re-check images after any compression or export step, since some tools shift white backgrounds by a few RGB points during save.

## FAQ

### Is 1000px still acceptable for Amazon main images in 2026?

A 1000px image on the longest side still enables Amazon's hover zoom feature, per Amazon's own documentation. It isn't rejected outright, but it leaves no margin for category-specific requirements or future resolution increases  -  which is why 2000px square is the safer working standard shown in this example's pass cases.

### What is the exact RGB value for the required white background?

Pure white is RGB 255, 255, 255 on all three channels. As SKU-04 and SKU-08 show, values like 254,254,250 or 250,250,250 look white on screen but fail an automated pixel check, since they're off by even a few points in one or more channels.

### Does the 85% fill rule apply to all product categories?

The general rule applies broadly, but some categories carry additional main image restrictions beyond fill percentage and background. Always check your specific category's requirements in Seller Central, since this worked example applies the general rule set only.

### Can I use a 3000x3000 image if I am targeting 2000px?

Yes, as long as the fill percentage still falls between 85% and 100% of the frame, like SKU-04 in the table. A larger canvas alone doesn't cause a failure  -  SKU-07 failed not because it was 3000x3000, but because the product only filled 70% of that larger frame.
